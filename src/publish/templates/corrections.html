Ask for a correction

We'd rather be corrected than be wrong in public. If something on this site is wrong, out of date, missing, or about you and shouldn't be here, tell us.

How to tell us

Open a correction request on GitHub. It's a short form, it's free, and you'll need a GitHub account to use it. You don't have to explain the technical side of anything.

Open a correction request

Not on GitHub and don't want to be? That's fine. You can read the whole project, including every issue people have raised, at our repository page, and the contact details for the maintainers are there too.

What to include

The more precise you are, the faster we can fix it. Useful things:

  • The address of the page (copy it from your browser's address bar).
  • The sentence or card you think is wrong.
  • What it should say instead, if you know.
  • A link to the document that shows it, if there is one.

If you can only manage "the bit about the licensing committee is wrong", send that. We'll work it out.

How long we take

We aim to respond within 10 working days. Straightforward factual fixes are usually much quicker than that. If a correction needs us to re-read a long document or go back to the council, we'll tell you that rather than leaving you waiting.

Every correction is logged in public on GitHub, so you can see what we changed and when.

If you're named on the site

Sometimes people appear in council documents by name. Councillors and senior officers, mostly, acting in their public roles. Occasionally an ordinary resident's name ends up in a published consultation response or a committee paper.

If you're named and you'd like it looked at, ask. Say which page and what you'd like us to do. We'll take out anything that isn't necessary for people to understand the decision, and if we decide something has to stay, we'll explain why rather than just refusing.

We only ever use documents the council has already published. But "already public" isn't the same as "fine to repeat", and we treat those as different questions.

What we won't do

We won't remove an accurate summary of a public decision because someone dislikes how it looks. We also won't take sides in a political argument about whether a decision was right. Our job stops at showing you the record and being honest about what it does and doesn't prove.

If you think we've crossed that line, say so in a correction request. That's exactly the sort of thing we want to hear about.

Before you write to us

Worth a look first: how we work explains where our summaries come from, what the coloured link labels mean, and why we so often say we don't know. A fair few disagreements turn out to be about that rather than about a fact.
