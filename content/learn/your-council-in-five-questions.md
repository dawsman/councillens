# Your council in five questions

Five questions, five documents, one evening. Works for any council in England, not only Norwich.
Answer them and you will know more about your local authority than most people who live there.

## 1. Which council actually does what, where I live?

Type your postcode into gov.uk's "find your local council". You will get either one council doing
everything, or two splitting the work.

In Norwich there are two. The city council does housing, bins, parks, planning and licensing. The
county does roads, schools and social care. Get this wrong and every complaint you send goes to the
wrong building.

**Where to look:** gov.uk, then the councils' own "what we do" pages.

## 2. Who represents me, and how did they vote?

Find your ward councillors by postcode on the council's website. Then find how they voted on
something that mattered, usually the budget, in the minutes of Full Council.

Norwich's 39 councillors voted on the 2026/27 budget on 24 February 2026. Opposition groups tabled
their own costed amendments, including £45,000 for tree planting at a stated £459 per tree. Those
amendments are published, which means you can see what each group would have done differently.

**Where to look:** the council's committee system, under Full Council.

## 3. How much do I pay, and who gets it?

Your bill shows the split. In Norwich, the city keeps about 13p in the pound, the county takes 73p
and the police take 14p.

Then check the trend. Norwich's share of a Band D bill went from £269.38 in 2020/21 to £315.26 in
2026/27, rising by 1.99% or 2.99% every single year, always just under the level that would trigger
a referendum.

**Where to look:** the February budget report, recommendations 1 and 2.

## 4. Did last year's plan survive?

Compare the budget with the outturn. Norwich's services overspent by £2.282m in 2025/26 while the
council overall finished about 1% under, rescued by an underspend on a central financing line.

Then check the building programme, which is where slippage hides. Norwich spent 64% of its five-year
capital budget to 2025/26.

**Where to look:** the outturn report to Cabinet, June or July.

## 5. What did the council promise, and what happened?

Pick one promise with a date on it. A policy due by a deadline, a report coming back to committee, a
scheme to be adopted.

Norwich's licensing policy has to be replaced by December 2026, five years after the last one. The
consultation closed on 9 August 2026 and the results were due at committee on 17 September. That is
a promise with a date, and anyone can check whether it is kept.

**Where to look:** committee reports, then the minutes of whichever meeting was supposed to follow.

## After the five

You now have the shape of the place: who does what, who decides, where the money comes from, whether
plans hold, and whether promises land.

If any answer surprises you, write to your councillor and ask about it. That is not a grand civic
gesture. It is an email, and the reply is often more useful than anything published.
