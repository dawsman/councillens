# Five classroom activities

Each activity sends students to a council document rather than to a worksheet. Every one has a
defensible answer and at least one honest disagreement in it. Timings assume a single lesson.

## 1. Grade the evidence yourself (25 minutes)

Open the Norwich licensing page on this site and find the link we labelled **yellow, possible**,
between the public consultation and the committee meeting due in September 2026. Read the quoted
sentence, then follow the link to the council's own document and read the paragraph around it.

Now decide. Would you call that link confirmed, possible, or no link found? Write one sentence
giving your reason.

Compare with ours. Where a group disagrees with our label, that is the lesson, not a mistake. Ask
what extra sentence in the record would have to exist for the label to move to green.

*Covers: evidence and inference, holding power to account.*

## 2. Overspent and underspent at the same time (20 minutes)

Norwich's services spent £2.282m more than budgeted in 2025/26. The council as a whole finished the
year about 1% under budget.

Both statements are true. In pairs, work out how, then check your answer against the outturn report
that went to Cabinet in June 2026. Look for a line called Corporate Financing.

Second question, harder: services have now overspent three years running, and each year the same
central line has covered it. Is that good management or a budget that does not describe reality?
Argue it both ways using only the council's figures.

*Covers: how councils are funded, reading financial information critically.*

## 3. The consultation that flipped (30 minutes)

Norwich consults on its council tax rise every year. For six years at least half of respondents
backed the rise. In 2026/27, out of 899 responses, 28% supported it and 48% opposed. The council
adopted 2.99% anyway.

Split the class. One half argues the council ignored the public. The other argues it acted properly.
Both sides may use only the budget report itself, which explains the referendum limit and the legal
duty to set a balanced budget.

Then vote, and ask whether anyone changed their mind because of a document rather than a speech.

*Covers: democracy and participation, limits on local power.*

## 4. Find the missing document (20 minutes)

In March 2026 Norwich officers told the licensing committee that a cumulative impact assessment
would be carried out alongside the consultation. We looked for it and found nothing published.

Give students fifteen minutes on the council's website to prove us wrong. They must record what
they searched for and where.

Whatever the result, ask the honest question: does "we could not find it" mean it does not exist?
Write the fairest one-line conclusion the class can agree on.

*Covers: research skills, the difference between absence of evidence and evidence of absence.*

## 5. Write a response that gets used (35 minutes)

Norwich consulted on five changes to who gets a council home. Agreement ran from 71% on the clearest
change down to 37% on giving armed forces veterans priority within a band.

Pick one change. Read the draft policy and the equality assessment in the Cabinet papers. Write 150
words as if responding to the consultation: say which proposal, give a reason, name who is affected,
and offer an alternative.

Then read them aloud and pick the three an officer could most easily summarise for councillors. That
is the real test, and it is not the same as the most passionate one.

*Covers: taking citizenship action, communicating with decision-makers.*
