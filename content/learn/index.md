# Learn

Local government is the layer of politics you actually bump into. Bins, parks, housing, the licence
that lets the chip shop open at midnight. It is also the layer almost nobody is taught about,
because national politics takes up all the room.

These eight pages fix that. Each one takes about five minutes. Each is built around real documents
from Norwich City Council that you can open in another tab and check for yourself, which is the
whole point: by the end you should not need us.

And then: [five classroom activities](classroom-activities.md), each one sending students to a real
council document rather than to us.

## For teachers

We wrote this against what the curriculum already asks for, rather than inventing a new subject.

**Key Stage 3 Citizenship** covers the roles of local government and how citizens take part in
democracy. Pages 1, 2 and 8 map onto that directly, and page 6 gives students a live example of
participation with a real closing date.

**GCSE Citizenship Studies** goes further: how local government is funded and how it spends, how
decisions are taken and challenged, and how citizens hold power to account. Pages 3, 4 and 5 are
the funding and decision-making content. The activities are designed to feed a Citizenship Action
project, because they produce evidence a student gathered rather than a worksheet they filled in.

**A-level Politics and BTEC Public Services** students can use the same material for democracy and
participation, and for pressure groups and accountability. The Norwich consultation results in
activity 3 make an unusually clean case study: six years where most respondents backed a council
tax rise, then one where most opposed it and the figure did not move.

None of this needs a subscription, a login, or anything from us. Every document we point at is
published by the council on its own website.

## A word on what we are not

We do not tell anyone whether a council is doing a good job. We show you what it said it would do,
what it did, and where the record goes quiet. Where the evidence does not support a conclusion, we
say so and leave it there.

That is a slightly annoying habit in a teacher and a necessary one in a source. Argue with us using
the documents, and if you find we have something wrong, [tell us](../../corrections/index.html).
